In 1867, Scottish planter James Taylor cleared 19 acres here and planted Sri Lanka's first commercial tea bushes — a decision that, within a few decades, turned the entire hill country economy toward tea and shaped the landscape you see on every Pekoe Trail stage today.

## What's there now

The estate is still active, growing and processing tea in a much smaller, more traditional operation than the large factories near Nuwara Eliya. A marker at the original planting site commemorates Taylor's work.

## Practical notes

There's no formal visitor center, so a local guide or estate contact is genuinely useful here — this is exactly the kind of stop worth arranging in advance through a Pekoe Trail guide rather than showing up expecting a tourist setup.
