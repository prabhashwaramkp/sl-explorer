Yala is Sri Lanka's most famous park for good reason — it holds one of the highest leopard densities recorded anywhere on Earth, alongside elephants, sloth bears, crocodiles, and over 200 bird species, all within a landscape of dry scrub, lagoons, and rocky outcrops.

## What to expect

Most visits are via open-top jeep safari, typically 3-4 hours, either at dawn or late afternoon when animals are most active. Block 1 has the highest sighting rates but also the most vehicle traffic — some operators specialize in less-crowded alternative routes if that matters to you.

## When to go

The dry season (roughly June to September) concentrates animals around remaining waterholes, generally improving sighting odds. The park closes for a short period annually (usually September) for conservation management — check current dates before planning around this window.

## Booking a safari

Safaris are typically arranged through licensed operators near the park entrance or your accommodation — jeep and guide costs are usually bundled together per vehicle rather than per person, so group sizes affect per-person cost significantly.
