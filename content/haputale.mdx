Haputale sits higher and cooler than Ella, with a quieter, more working-town feel — this is tea-estate country in a more literal sense, with active plantations right up to the edge of town rather than a backpacker strip built around them.

## What Haputale is actually like

Mist rolls through often, even at midday, and the wind-swept ridgelines here give some of the widest panoramic views anywhere in the hill country. It's a good base for 2-3 days of trekking and waterfall-hopping without needing to relocate.

## What to do here

- **[Pekoe Trail Stages 12-14](/pekoe-trail-stage-12-haputale)** — three consecutive stages through ridge, cloud forest, and highland terrain
- **[Lipton's Seat](/liptons-seat)** — the panoramic viewpoint where Sir Thomas Lipton reportedly surveyed his tea estates
- **[Diyaluma Falls](/diyaluma-falls)** — Sri Lanka's second-highest waterfall, with natural pools at the top
- **[Bambarakanda Falls](/bambarakanda-falls)** — the island's tallest waterfall, a short easy walk from the road

## Where to go next

From Haputale, **[Horton Plains and World's End](/horton-plains-worlds-end)** is a natural next stop — the highland plateau and its dramatic escarpment sit close enough to combine into a longer highland trip before continuing on toward Nuwara Eliya.
